---
title: Power Chords
summary: A root and a fifth. Two notes that carry rock, punk and metal.
prereqs: [note-names-low-e]
---

A power chord is a root plus the note a **fifth** above it. In E5 the root is E and the fifth is B. On guitar that is the open low E string plus the second fret of the A string:

```
e|---
B|---
G|---
D|---
A|--2--   <- fifth (B)
E|--0--   <- root  (E)
```

Because a power chord has no third, it is neither major nor minor. That is why it works everywhere in rock: move the same shape up the low strings and you get G5, A5, and so on.

Two shapes cover most of what you need on the E and A strings:

| Chord | Low E string | A string |
|-------|--------------|----------|
| E5    | open (0)     | 2        |
| G5    | 3            | 5        |
| A5    | 5            | 7        |

Keep the shape locked and move it as a block. The picking hand does the rhythm; the fretting hand only has to find the next two numbers.

The **fifth** is also the first step away from the major scale, which you will meet in the theory track. For now, hear it: it sounds stable, open, and loud.
